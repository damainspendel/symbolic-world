# E5b — Expanded voice-specialist calibration (paired, n=25+25)

**Date:** 2026-07-27 · **Design:** the 25 voice-flip corruptions and 25 clean controls
from E1b, re-judged by the narrow voice specialist (same items the generalist judged
blind in E1b — a true paired comparison). Files: `pipeline/work/e5b_*`.

| | Specialist | Generalist (E1b, same items) |
|---|---|---|
| Voice-flip sensitivity | **20/25 (80%, CI 61–91%)** | 18/25 (72%, CI 52–86%) |
| Specificity | **24/25 (96%)** | 93% (on the full E1b control set) |

**Paired discordance (the honest core):** both caught 17 · specialist-only **3** ·
generalist-only **1** · neither 4. McNemar discordant pairs 3:1 — directionally
favoring the specialist, not statistically significant at this n.

**Conclusion:** across now five measurements (E1, E1b, E5, E5b, and the cross-vendor
replay) the specialist is never worse and usually better on the voice axis, at high
specificity and lower cost per judgment — but the effect is modest (+8pp here), and
the four never-caught items are the familiar sympathetic-reportage boundary that
resists the taxonomy itself. The strong early claim ("doubled detection") does not
survive larger samples; the defensible claim is: a narrow brief buys a small, cheap,
consistent improvement — and the residual is a property of the text, not the prompt.
